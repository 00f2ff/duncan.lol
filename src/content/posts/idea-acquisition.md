I read somewhere that engineers can now write code so quickly that the current bottleneck in software creation is idea generation. I don't feel like I've run out of ideas, but I'm certainly being more intentional about what I read and take notes on. Effective use of AI can be a leverage multiplier, and given the shifts I'm observing in organizational behavior and the nature of work for tech professionals, mindful application of an interesting idea can be particularly impactful.


### Organizational behavior


Junior engineers are no longer tapping senior engineers for assistance or to ask questions; they're just asking AI. AI answers the questions and it's not intimidating. And I'm sure it often communicates more succinctly, accurately, and politely than senior engineers might. But continuous use of AI causes the user to develop a peculiar sense of taste for what good software looks like. This lack of interpersonal communication atrophies the throughways of tribal knowledge, and I want to avoid a world ten years from now where all senior software engineers think the same way. That would be rather bland, and is a disservice to creativity.


### Nature of work


Historically, software engineers have used more or less the same set of tools as one another, with varying degrees of sophistication based on personal expertise. Today the tools are mostly AI-related, but there are three major differences now:

1. How well you use an individual instance of that tool. That is based on taste. Taste, I believe, is the best description of what is sometimes referred to as _experience_ for software engineers.
2. How willing you are to disrupt preconceived notions of what being a software engineer is. What your openness is to experimentation with different modes of working, be it trying to achieve rapid velocity with an agent as a tool or an agent as a thought partner or multiple agents.
3. Whether you value context switching. If you are willing to evaluate working with multiple agents concurrently with some degree of human-in-the-loop, there's a premium on the ability to context switch. Historically, context switching has been frowned upon as it disrupts deep work. A lot of deep work can now be exported, but human attention and quality control still matters.

### Constructing social constructs


Since the advent of these new AI coding paradigms, I've been increasingly interested in how you preserve and improve organizations. I've been reading more about how individuals and corporations run individual agents and sets of agents together. We've moved from human-human and machine-machine knowledge exchange that is mostly disjoint to a matrix in which humans also share knowledge with machines and vice versa. 


We haven't reached AGI, and as much as LLMs sessions are born knowing, they are basically stateless. The way that engineers have been using them to conduct work has essentially created miniature ephemeral societies of humans and agents working collectively in the context of one another. It's like the Garden of Eden but the apple is burning $10,000 in tokens.


### I am but a dry sponge


I've been trying to read a lot about different kinds of intelligences and emergent structures and systems, from biology and physics to software engineering and math. I realize that I should also read more psychology, sociology and cultural anthropology.


When I walk and run, I've been paying more attention to how things look and work. I try to pattern match on latent interconnectedness in the world around me. 


My hope is that by letting my mind weave around these concepts more, eventually I'll have the right stuff for a nice little epiphany. I'd love to change my mind about something; to uncover a way to build better systems, experiences or software; to enable my team to produce work that's better than anything they've ever done before. 


If you've learned anything interesting lately, I'd love to hear it.
